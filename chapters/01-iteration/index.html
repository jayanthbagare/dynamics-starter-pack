Iteration & fixed points

In Chapter 0 the calculator’s tape told the story one number at a time. Here is a picture that shows the whole story at once, the cobweb, and a single number that predicts how it ends: the slope at the fixed point.

From presses to a picture

Below is the graph of y = \cos x and the dashed diagonal y = x. The ▲ on the axis is where you start. Press Step and follow the path. It alternates between two moves:

  • up (or down) to the curve: apply the rule, like pressing the button;
  • across to the diagonal: the output becomes the next input.

Drag the ▲ to start somewhere else. Hover over the time series to find each press on the cobweb.

Fixed points live on the diagonal

A fixed point is a number the rule gives back unchanged: f(x^*) = x^*. On the cobweb that means the curve meets the diagonal. That’s where the path stops moving. Switch buttons, and drag the start across each fixed point.

For x², start just below 1 and then just above it. The same fixed point sends you two different ways: it’s hollow.

The slope dial

Why does cos pull you in while x² pushes you off 1? Strip the question down to the simplest possible rule: a straight line through a fixed point at 0.5. The only thing left to choose is its slope m. Grab the ◆ knob on the line, or use the slider.

Two things decide everything:

  • the size of the slope: |m| < 1 shrinks the distance to the fixed point every step, while |m| > 1 stretches it;
  • the sign of the slope: positive keeps you on one side (a staircase), while negative flips you across each step (a spiral).

At exactly |m| = 1 the dial sits on the fence. The dot turns half-filled: neither pulled in nor pushed away.

Zoom in: every curve is a line up close

A real rule like cos isn’t a straight line. But zoom in far enough on a fixed point and the curve becomes indistinguishable from its tangent line (dashed). The tangent’s slope, f'(x^*), is the slope dial’s m.

Near a fixed point, every rule behaves like the slope dial set to the curve’s own slope there. So you don’t need to iterate to know the ending: read off the slope. Small slope, pulled in. Steep slope, pushed away. Negative slope, you alternate sides.

That’s why cos alternated above and below 0.739 on the calculator tape: its slope there is negative.

Crank the zoom to ×32 on any case. The curve and the dashed tangent merge, and the cobweb inside looks exactly like the slope dial at m = f'(x^*).

Write the distance from the fixed point as \eta_n = x_n - x^*. Then

\eta_{n+1} = f(x^* + \eta_n) - x^* \approx f'(x^*)\,\eta_n,

so near x^* each step multiplies the distance by \lambda = f'(x^*), and

|\lambda| < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{stable}, \qquad |\lambda| > 1 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{unstable}.

When |\lambda| = 1 the linear approximation can’t decide, and the neglected curvature does.
