Bifurcations in 1D flows

Turn a dial on a flow and, at special values, fixed points appear, vanish or trade places. On the line there are only a few ways this can happen, and each has a picture: a landscape that tilts until its valleys change.

Landscapes

Every flow on the line can be pictured as a ball in a very sticky landscape: it always moves downhill, and stops the moment the ground is flat. The height of the landscape is the potential V(x), with

\dot x = f(x) = -\frac{dV}{dx}.

Valleys are stable fixed points and hilltops are unstable ones. The 3D view shows the landscape for every value of the dial r at once, front to back. The highlighted slice is the current r, and its ball is where the system is. Drag the view to turn it. Click the phase line below it to drop more balls.

Saddle-node: a valley disappears

\dot x = r + x^2. For r < 0 there’s a valley and a hill. As r rises they slide together, merge into a flat shelf at r = 0 (half-stable), and then there’s nothing left.

Just past r = 0 the valley is gone, but the ball still dawdles where it used to be: the ghost of the fixed point. The smaller r is, the longer the bottleneck.

Transcritical: two fixed points trade places

\dot x = r x - x^2. Here x = 0 is a fixed point for every r, so it can’t vanish. Instead, a second fixed point, at x = r, passes through it.

Pitchfork, and tilting it

\dot x = r x - x^3 is symmetric: flip x \to -x and nothing changes. As r passes 0, one valley becomes two, a mirror pair.

Real systems are never perfectly symmetric. The tilt slider adds a term h: \dot x = h + r x - x^3. With any tilt at all, the pitchfork breaks. One branch now joins up smoothly, and the other appears on its own through a saddle-node.

Hysteresis: the system remembers

\dot x = r x + x^3 - x^5, a subcritical pitchfork. Past r = 0 the ball doesn’t ease away from 0: it jumps to a far valley. Use Sweep up and Sweep down and watch its path on the diagram below.

Solid = stable, dotted = unstable (Strogatz’s solid/dashed). The blue line is where the ball has been.

On the line, fixed points can only change in a few ways: a valley and a hill merge and vanish (saddle-node), pass through each other and trade stability (transcritical), or one valley splits in two (pitchfork). Everything else is one of these in disguise.

Turn the 3D view to look straight down: the floor under the surface is the bifurcation diagram for the current form. Stable branches run along the valley floors, and unstable branches along the ridges.

The normal forms and their potentials (f = -V'):

\begin{aligned} \text{saddle-node:}\ & \dot x = r + x^2, & V &= -r x - \tfrac{x^3}{3}\\ \text{transcritical:}\ & \dot x = r x - x^2, & V &= -\tfrac{r x^2}{2} + \tfrac{x^3}{3}\\ \text{pitchfork:}\ & \dot x = r x - x^3, & V &= -\tfrac{r x^2}{2} + \tfrac{x^4}{4}\\ \text{subcritical:}\ & \dot x = r x + x^3 - x^5, & V &= -\tfrac{r x^2}{2} - \tfrac{x^4}{4} + \tfrac{x^6}{6} \end{aligned}
