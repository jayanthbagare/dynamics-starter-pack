Flows on the line

So far the rules have jumped: x₀, then x₁, then x₂. Most of physics doesn’t jump, it glides. This chapter makes that switch. It’s also where the book’s own vocabulary begins.

A flow on the line

A map says where to jump next. A flow says how fast to move right now:

\dot x = f(x).

The dot means “rate of change in time”. The graph below plots that speed f(x) against position x. The horizontal axis is also the line the particles live on, the phase line. Click anywhere on the plot to drop a particle onto the line (Shift-click drops a whole row).

Drag f(x)

Now shape the rule yourself. Drag the ◆ handles and watch the arrows and fixed points follow. The particles you dropped above keep flowing under whatever rule you draw.

Fixed points are where the speed is zero, where the graph meets the axis. If the graph goes downhill through the axis (+ on the left, − on the right), both sides flow in: stable. Uphill, both sides flow out: unstable. If the graph only touches the axis, one side flows in and the other flows out: half-stable.

A particle can only ever move one way, and it can’t cross a fixed point. So on the line it either settles or runs off. It never oscillates.

Look at the arrows next to each dot: two arrows pointing in means a filled dot, two pointing out means hollow, and one of each means half-filled. The time series above only ever rise or fall, then flatten.

Near a fixed point, f(x) \approx f'(x^*)(x - x^*), so the distance \eta = x - x^* obeys \dot\eta \approx f'(x^*)\,\eta and \eta(t) \approx \eta_0\, e^{f'(x^*)\,t}. Compare with Chapter 1:

Euler is iteration

A computer can’t glide. It takes small steps of time h: move at the current speed for a moment, then look again.

x_{n+1} = x_n + h\,f(x_n).

That’s a map, so everything from Chapters 1–3 applies. Here it is for logistic growth, \dot x = x(1 - x), whose true solution climbs smoothly to 1 and stops.

The real flow can never oscillate. Every overshoot, cycle and bit of chaos you see here comes from the step size, not from the flow.
